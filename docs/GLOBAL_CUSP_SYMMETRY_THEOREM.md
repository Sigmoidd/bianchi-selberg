# Global cusp symmetry for Γ₀(2+i): theorems and proofs

Branch `research/global-cusp-symmetry`, base commit `f233ef4ba7cde6e9811cd375e5135e10afaafdf3`.
Nothing here asserts a new eigenvalue, spectral gap, or completed global certificate.

Tier labels: **PROVED** (exact algebra, or a complete argument from standard theorems that are
named), **CONDITIONAL** (correct deduction from an explicit uncertified input),
**EXPERIMENTAL** (numerical diagnostic).  Exact algebra is replayed by
`global_cusp_character_algebra.py`, `global_quarter_turn.py`, `global_conjugate_prime.py`,
`global_oldspace.py`; tests in `tests/test_global_cusp_symmetry.py`.

## 0. Setting and conventions

K = ℚ(i), 𝒪 = ℤ[i], π = 2+i, π̄ = 2−i, Γ = PSL₂(ℤ[i]), H_π = Γ₀(π), X = H_π\ℍ³ (volume
6·vol(Γ\ℍ³)).  Cosets H_π\Γ ↔ ℙ¹(𝔽₅), 𝔽₅ = 𝒪/π with i ↦ 3.  Labels follow the repository and are
*derived* in code, not assumed: label 0 = H_π·I, label 1+k = H_π·S·T₁ᵏ (k = 0..4); a coset is the
bottom row (c₁:c₂) mod π; right action c ↦ c·g; forward permutations `FWD[g]` equal the inverse of
the stored `GLUE[g]`.  Pullback representation (ρ(g)v)_c = v_{c·g}, so ρ(gh) = ρ(g)ρ(h).  A
function f on X corresponds to F_c(z) = f(γ_c z) with F(γz) = ρ(γ)F(z).

Cusps of X: ∞ (cross-section lattice Λ_∞ = 𝒪) and 0 (scaling S, Λ₀ = π𝒪, covolume 5, dual lattice
(π/5)𝒪 = (1/π̄)𝒪).  Verified exactly in `global_quarter_turn.check_cusp_lattices`.

## 1. Local-to-global obstruction (Workstream A) — PROVED

**Proposition A1 (finite algebra).**
(a) ρ(Γ) is the order-60 group generated by the four repository permutations; its class sizes are
1,12,12,15,20, no union of classes other than {1} and everything has order dividing 60, so it is
simple of order 60, i.e. ≅ A₅ ≅ PSL₂(𝔽₅); all elements are even permutations of the 6 points.
(b) It is doubly transitive (point stabilizer of order 10); ⟨χ,χ⟩ = 2, ⟨χ,1⟩ = 1, so ℂ⁶ = **1** ⊕ V₅
with V₅ irreducible, and the commutant of ρ(Γ) in M₆(ℚ) is exactly span{I, J} (J = all-ones),
computed by exact linear algebra.
(c) Σ_{j=0}^{4} ζ₅^{kj} = 0 for k = 1,…,4 and = 5 for k = 0 (exact in ℚ(ζ₅) = ℚ[x]/Φ₅).
(d) A_T = ⅕ Σ_j ρ(T₁)^j is a rational symmetric idempotent of rank 2, equal to the repository's
singular projector; I−A_T has rank 4 and equals the span of the four characters
w_k = (0, ζᵏ⁰, ζᵏ¹, …, ζᵏ⁴), ρ(T₁)w_k = ζᵏ w_k.  The point reflection R (z ↦ −z) satisfies
ρ(R)w_k = w_{5−k}.
(e) [A_T, ρ(S)] ≠ 0 (explicit matrix in `global_cusp_character_algebra_result.json`); A_T commutes
with ρ(T₁), ρ(R), ρ(T_iR) but not ρ(S).  The group-orbit of the 4-dim regular subspace spans
V₅ (dimension 5), so no 4-dimensional subspace invariant under the whole group contains it.

**Lemma A2 (no constant fiber operator).** Let M be a smooth section of End(E_ρ) and let [M]
denote multiplication by M on sections.  Then [M] commutes with the Laplacian on C_c^∞ iff M is
parallel, iff M ∈ commutant(ρ(Γ)) = span{I, J}.
*Proof.* In the flat trivialization over ℍ³ (F equivariant, Δ componentwise),
[Δ,M]F = (ΔM)F + 2⟨∇M,∇F⟩.  Equivariant F can be chosen with arbitrary 1-jet at any point
whose neighborhood injects into X, so vanishing of the first-order term forces ∇M = 0; M is then
constant on ℍ³ and equivariance gives M ρ(γ) = ρ(γ) M.  ∎
**Corollary A3.** If E = xI + yJ/6 kills both singular directions e₀ and e₁+…+e₅, comparing the
e₁-coordinate of E e₀ gives y = 0 and then x = 0.  Hence no nonzero globally equivariant constant
fiber operator annihilates the two singular cusp directions; the cusp-local projector A_T is not a
global bundle endomorphism (it fails to commute with ρ(S)).  Any global cuspidal projector
commuting with Δ must therefore use *base* isometries, which is what §2 does.

## 2. The quarter turn (Workstream B)

Q = diag(i,1) ∈ PGL₂(𝒪) acts on ℍ³ by Q(z,y) = (iz,y) (|det| = 1, exact in
`global_cusp_gaussian.h3_action`).  det Q = i is not a square of a unit, so Q ∉ PSL₂(𝒪): it
represents the nontrivial coset of PGL₂(𝒪)/PSL₂(𝒪) and is a genuine extra symmetry.

**Theorem B1 (normalization, involution) — PROVED.** Conjugation γ ↦ QγQ⁻¹ maps
[[a,b],[c,d]] ↦ [[a,ib],[−ic,d]].  This preserves integrality and determinant 1, and the lower-left
entry −ic is divisible by π iff c is, so Q normalizes Γ and H_π.  Q² = diag(−1,1) is, as an
isometry, z ↦ −z, represented by diag(i,−i) = R ∈ H_π, and conjugation by Q² equals conjugation
by R.  Hence Q induces an involutive isometry of the orbifold X, and U_Q f = f∘Q is a unitary
involution of L²(X).
**Theorem B2 (commutes with Δ) — PROVED.** Δ is essentially self-adjoint on C_c^∞(X) (X complete),
U_Q maps C_c^∞(X) to itself and commutes with Δ there, hence with its closure; U_Q preserves
Dom(Δ), and so do P_± = (I ± U_Q)/2, which are orthogonal projectors commuting with Δ.

**Theorem B3 (both cusps) — PROVED.** (i) At ∞, σ_∞ = I and U_Q rotates the horizontal
coordinate by i.  (ii) At 0, σ₀ = S and Q S = S Q₀ with Q₀(z,y) = (−iz,y) (exact check on
points; algebraically diag(i,1)S = S·diag(1,i)), so the horizontal coordinate rotates by −i.
Both lattices 𝒪 and π𝒪 are stable under multiplication by ±i, and rotation is a Lebesgue-measure
preserving automorphism of ℂ/Λ_c.  Write CT_c f(y) = vol(ℂ/Λ_c)⁻¹ ∫_{ℂ/Λ_c} f(σ_c(z,y)) dx.  Then
CT_c(U_Q f) = CT_c(f) for c ∈ {∞, 0}, for f ∈ L²(X) (Fubini; defined for a.e. y).
Equivalently on Fourier modes: φ_μ(iz,y) = φ_{−iμ}(z,y) at ∞ and the same with frequency
πβ/5 at 0 (exact integer identity ⟨μ,ix⟩ = ⟨−iμ,x⟩).

**Theorem B4 (the odd range is cuspidal) — PROVED, modulo the named standard theorem in (b).**
Let L²_cusp = {f ∈ L²(X): CT_∞ f = CT_0 f = 0}.
(a) Ran P₋ ⊆ L²_cusp, because CT_c(P₋f) = ½(CT_c f − CT_c f) = 0 at both cusps.
(b) Δ restricted to L²_cusp has purely discrete spectrum (Gelfand–Graev–Piatetski-Shapiro /
Selberg; for the Bianchi setting see Elstrodt–Grunewald–Mennicke, *Groups Acting on Hyperbolic
Space*, Ch. 6.  I cite at chapter level and have not pinned the theorem number).  Since
L²_odd = Ran P₋ reduces Δ, Δ|_odd is the restriction of a discrete-spectrum operator to a reducing
subspace, hence has discrete spectrum.
(c) *Independent proof of (a) without constant terms.* Q fixes each cusp as a point and normalizes
H_π, so it normalizes each cusp stabilizer Γ_c, and σ_c⁻¹Qσ_c is a rotation about the vertical
axis.  Reindexing Γ_c\H_π by γ ↦ Q⁻¹γQ gives E_c(QP,s) = E_c(P,s) for the Eisenstein series, and
likewise for their residues (including the constants).  So every Eisenstein wave packet and the
residual spectrum lie in L²_even, and L²_odd ⊥ them; by the Roelcke–Selberg decomposition
L²_odd ⊆ L²_cusp.  This kills the full continuous and residual spectrum, not selected Fourier
coefficients.
Not claimed: that the odd sector is nonempty or what its eigenvalues are.

## 3. Bundle-level action and fifth-root reduction (Workstream C) — PROVED

**Theorem C1.** Let σ be the permutation of cosets defined by Qγ_cQ⁻¹ ∈ H_π γ_{σ(c)}.  From the
bottom-row rule (c₁:c₂) ↦ (−ic₁:c₂) with i ≡ 3, one gets σ = q = (0,1,4,2,5,3) (forward
convention; fixes labels 0 and 1; 4-cycle 2→4→5→3→2), which agrees with the candidate in the
handoff.  Then
 (U_Q F)(z) = ρ_q F(Qz),  (ρ_q v)_c = v_{q(c)}
maps equivariant F to equivariant F (this needs ρ_q ρ(QγQ⁻¹) ρ_q⁻¹ = ρ(γ) for all γ, verified
on generators, T_i, and 80 random words), and 𝒬² F(z) = ρ_q² F(Q²z) = ρ_q² ρ(R) F(z) = F(z)
because q² = ρ(R) as permutations and ρ(R)² = I.  So the fiber map q alone is *not* an involution
(q² = ρ(R) ≠ I); only the pair (q, base map Q) is.
**Convention warning.** As *permutations* (functions, composition p∘p′) q∘T₁∘q⁻¹ = T₁³, matching
the handoff.  As *matrices in the repository's pullback convention* ρ_qρ(T₁)ρ_q⁻¹ = ρ(T₁)² (the
exponent is 3⁻¹ mod 5), which is the form that the equivariance computation above needs
(Q T₁ Q⁻¹ = T_i and ρ(T_i) = ρ(T₁)³).
**Proposition C2.** q lies outside the order-60 image (it is an odd permutation), conjugation by q
normalizes the image on all four generators including S, and ⟨image, q⟩ has order 120 (PGL₂(𝔽₅)).
ρ_q w_k = w_{3k}, so q permutes the four nontrivial characters in one orbit (1→3→4→2→1).

**What this does and does not reduce.** The four fifth-root modes lie in a single q-orbit, but the
global symmetry is U_Q, not q.  On Fourier–Whittaker coefficients a_μ (cusp ∞) and b_β (cusp 0,
frequency πβ/5), f ∈ L²_ε (U_Q f = εf) iff a_{iν} = ε a_ν and b_{iβ} = ε b_β.  The lattice
rotation ν ↦ iν acts freely on nonzero modes, so each C₄-orbit contributes one unknown.  Exact
counts for the repository's mode boxes: M = 1: 24 → 6; M = 4: 80 → 20 (reduction factor exactly
4; the existing point reflection R alone gives 2).  This reduces linear-algebra *dimension*; it
does **not** reduce the number of distinct Arb K_{ir} evaluations (rotation-equivalent modes
share |μ| and are already merged by the Bessel cache: M = 4 uses 204 distinct evaluations for 80
unknowns).  No per-character block reuse is claimed: the existing system encodes k through the
six copies at translations, and the reduction is on coefficient space.

## 4. Conjugate prime (Workstream D) — PROVED

C(z,y) = (z̄,y) is an orientation-reversing isometry with C(γp) = γ̄ C(p), so C H_π C⁻¹ = H_π̄
(lower-left entry c̄ divisible by π̄ iff c by π; the groups differ, e.g. [[1,0],[π̄,1]] ∈ H_π̄∖H_π).
It carries the zero-cusp lattice π𝒪 to π̄𝒪 and the dual (π/5)𝒪 to (π̄/5)𝒪; zero-cusp frequency
conjugates as freq_π(β̄) = conj(freq_π̄(β)).  C Q C⁻¹ = Q⁻¹ (exact, on ℍ³), and Q⁻¹ = Q as
operators on X since Q² ∈ H_π.
**Theorem D1.** U_C f = f∘C is a unitary L²(H_π̄\ℍ³) → L²(H_π\ℍ³) with U_C Δ = Δ U_C and
U_C P₋^{(π̄)} = P₋^{(π)} U_C; f ↦ conj(f∘C) is the antiunitary version.  Hence Δ on X_π and X_π̄
have the same spectrum with multiplicity, also in the odd sectors.  Conjugate-prime eigenvalues
are one discovery, not two.
**Distinction.** At fiber level, with the same labels, ρ_π̄(ḡ) = ρ_π(g) exactly (T₁, S, R have equal
permutations for π and π̄; T_i ↦ T₁³ for π and T₁² for π̄), so the fiber intertwiner is the identity
matrix and Gaussian conjugation leaves every T₁-character label k fixed.  The quarter-turn twist
acts on labels as k ↦ 3k (π) and k ↦ 2k = (3k)⁻¹ (π̄) (q̄ = q⁻¹).  Cyclotomic Galois relabelings
σ_a: ζ₅ ↦ ζ₅^a are relabelings of coefficients, not isometries; they are related to q but are not
the action of C.

## 5. Old/new (Workstream E)

Lifts B₁f = f and B_π f = f∘g, g = diag(π,1).  *PROVED (exact):* gMg⁻¹ = [[a,πb],[c/π,d]] ∈ Γ iff
π | c, so gH_πg⁻¹ ⊂ Γ and f∘g is H_π-automorphic; in the six-copy picture F2_c(z) = f(gγ_c z)
satisfies the same equivariance as the first lift; the six g γ_c lie in six distinct left
Γ-cosets (Hecke decomposition), so Σ_c f(gγ_c z) = (T_π f)(z); Q commutes with g, so
U_Q B_j = B_j U_Q^{(1)} for both lifts; the Fricke element W = S g normalizes H_π, f∘W = f∘g for
Γ-invariant f, and W⁻¹QWQ⁻¹ = diag(−i,i) ∈ H_π, so U_W commutes with U_Q.
**Corollary E1 (the invariant line is not the oldspace).** The old space of a level-one f is
spanned by f and f∘g; the second lift has trivial-fiber component (τ/6)f (τ the T_π eigenvalue)
plus a V₅ component.  The line **1** ⊂ ℂ⁶ is the span of the first lift only.
**Definition.** P_old,full is the orthogonal projector onto the closure of Ran B₁ + Ran B_π.
(The projector onto a closure always exists.)
**Theorem E2 — PROVED from E-algebra and B2.** (i) P_old,full commutes with U_Q (both ranges are
U_Q-stable and U_Q is unitary).  (ii) It commutes with Δ (B_j intertwine Δ).  (iii) P_new,₋ :=
(I−P_old,full)P₋ is an orthogonal projector (product of commuting orthogonal projectors),
commutes with Δ, and Ran P_new,₋ ⊆ Ran P₋ ⊆ L²_cusp.  (iv) Ran(P_old,full P₋) is the closure of the
lifts of Q-odd level-one functions; Q fixes the unique level-one cusp, so those are cuspidal by
the argument of B4.
**Gram data (CONDITIONAL on Hecke eigenfunction f, T_π f = τ f).** ‖f₁‖² = ‖f₂‖² = 6‖f‖²_Γ and
⟨f₁,f₂⟩ = ⟨f,T_πf⟩_Γ; since ‖T_π‖ ≤ 6 with equality only on functions invariant under the
non-discrete group ⟨Γ,g⟩ (constants), |τ| < 6 for non-constant f, so the two lifts are linearly
independent and the old space is 2-dimensional.  Closedness of Ran B₁ + Ran B_π with a uniform
angle would follow from a uniform bound |τ| ≤ 6 − δ on the non-constant spectrum (e.g. from a
bound toward Ramanujan, cited, not verified here).
**Status of newness: UNCERTIFIED.** P_old,full is not computable on a given trial without
certified level-one spectral data.  A new-space statement for an odd quasimode at λ would
need dist(λ, σ_odd(Δ_Γ)) > R₋/μ₋ for the Q-odd level-one spectrum; the repository has no such
certified data.  No newform claim is made anywhere.

## 6. The odd spectral enclosure (Workstream F, theorem part)

**Theorem F1 — PROVED.** Let W ∈ Dom(Δ) ⊂ L²(X) and W₋ = P₋W ≠ 0, λ ∈ ℝ.  Then
 dist(λ, σ(Δ|_odd)) ≤ R₋/μ₋,  R₋ = ‖(Δ−λ)W₋‖₂,  μ₋ = ‖W₋‖₂,
and σ(Δ|_odd) is purely discrete and cuspidal by B4.
*Proof.* W₋ ∈ Dom(Δ|_odd) and (Δ−λ)W₋ = P₋(Δ−λ)W by B2; apply the spectral-theorem bound
dist(λ,σ(A)) ≤ ‖(A−λ)u‖/‖u‖ to the self-adjoint operator A = Δ|_odd.  ∎
**Corollary F2.** R₋ = ‖P₋(Δ−λ)W‖ ≤ ‖(Δ−λ)W‖ = R exactly, so any certified bound on the
unprojected residual bounds the projected one for the same W.  The projected mass satisfies
μ₋² = ½(‖W‖² − Re⟨W, U_Q W⟩); it requires its own certificate (a Q-overlap integral for the
*same* W).  The enclosure radius R₋/μ₋ is not ordered against R/μ in general.  If W is exactly
Q-odd (W₋ = W) nothing is lost: R₋ = R and μ₋ = μ.
**Conditional statement F3.** The theorem requires W globally automorphic and in Dom(Δ).  The
Hejhal-gluing construction yields such a W with a defect bound R ≤ τ + ‖B₀d₀+B₁d₁‖₂.  Whether the
glued W is exactly odd depends on whether the partition of unity and gluing maps are Q-equivariant;
this has not been checked, so W₋ = P₋W must be treated as a new object whose mass needs its own
certificate.  The existing certificates (floor, projected mass) belong to a different trial
(r = 6.62208, M_∞ = 8, M_0 = 40) than the physical solve (r = 6.7439020359331625, M = 1) and cannot be
transferred; see the audit.

## 7. Scope: no generalization is claimed

The quarter turn Q = diag(i,1) is specific to ℤ[i]: Theorem B3 uses i𝒪 = 𝒪 and iπ𝒪 = π𝒪 at the
two cusps of Γ₀(2+i).  Nothing here is claimed for d = 11, 19, 43, 67, 163.  The *form* of the
argument (an isometry that normalizes the group, fixes every cusp class, and acts on each
cross-section lattice by a measure-preserving automorphism, so constant terms are preserved) is
general, and other isometries (for instance z ↦ −z at level one when d ≠ 1) may admit the same
argument.  Each such case needs its own proof that the specific subgroup is normalized, that
every cusp class is fixed with its lattice preserved, and a separate check that the odd sector is
what the certificate needs.  None of that is established or tested in this branch.
