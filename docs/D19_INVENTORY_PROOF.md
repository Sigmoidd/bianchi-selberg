## D19-L1: the base PID and all lattice types

The imaginary quadratic Minkowski bound is 2√19/π<3. The polynomial
X²−X+5 is irreducible modulo 2, so there is no prime ideal of norm 2.
Every ideal class has an integral ideal representative of norm at most 2,
and hence norm 1: O is a PID. This argument does not assert norm-Euclideanity.
The norm a²+ab+5b²=(a+b/2)²+19b²/4 gives O×={±1}.
Bezout completes primitive cusp pairs to SL₂(O); there is one cusp and
rotation index GG=1. Neither i nor ζ₆ is in K, so elliptics fix no rational
cusp; there are no cuspidal elliptics.

Real integral elliptic lift traces are 0,±1. Negating a lift leaves the
polynomials X²+1 and X²−X+1. For r=i or α=ζ₆, an action on O² is an
O[r]-stable lattice in the one-dimensional K(r) space. Intertwiners are
multiplication by K(r)×. Since O is a PID, these lattices are O-free;
GL₂(O) classes correspond to their ideal classes. The determinant of a
commuting unit is its relative norm.

A₂=O[i] and A₃=O[α] are maximal orders: the discriminant pairs (−19,−4)
and (−19,−3) are coprime. Locally at least one factor is unramified, whose
tensor with the other integer ring is a product of unramified DVR extensions,
hence integrally closed. Their basis (1,τ,r,τr) discriminants are 5776=76²
and 3249=57². There are no conductor lattice types.

The quartic CM Minkowski bound is 3√|disc|/(2π²). We prove both class
numbers one by treating every prime ideal norm below the bound.

### A₂: 114/π²<12

Representatives have norm ≤11. The only possible prime ideal norms in this
range are 4,5,9, as follows.

- At 2, the base is unramified with residue F₄. The polynomial for i−1 is
  Y²+2Y+2, Eisenstein. There is one norm-4 prime, generated by 1+i, whose
  absolute norm is 4. No norm-2 prime exists.
- At 3, the base is inert, with residue F₉. The two roots of i are
  1+τ and −1−τ in F₉. There are two norm-9 primes. The elements
  (2+τ)+(3−τ)i and (2+τ)−(3−τ)i have absolute norm 9 and vanish on
  distinct factors; both primes are principal. No norm-3 prime exists.
- At 5, τ has residues 0,1 and i has residues 2,3. The elements
  2±τi and 2±(1−τ)i have absolute norm 5; each vanishes at exactly one
  of the four different residue pairs, exhausting the norm-5 primes.
- At 7 and 11, the base splits (τ residues 2,6 and 3,9 respectively),
  but X²+1 is irreducible in F₇ and F₁₁. Prime norms are 49 and 121,
  outside the bound.

All prime factors of any norm-≤11 representative are therefore principal.
Composite norms introduce no additional classes. Hence h(A₂)=1.

### A₃: 171/(2π²)<9

Representatives have norm ≤8. The only possible prime ideal norms are 4,7.

- At 2, α²−α+1 splits over F₄ with roots τ and τ+1. The elements
  (1+τ)+(2−τ)α and 3+(−2+τ)α have absolute norm 4 and vanish on
  distinct factors. Both norm-4 primes are principal; none has norm 2.
- At 3, the base is inert; α ramifies by Y=α+1, whose polynomial is
  Y²−3Y+3, Eisenstein. Its prime norm is 9, outside the bound.
- At 5, the base splits but α²−α+1 is irreducible over F₅: prime norms
  25 are outside the bound.
- At 7, τ has residues 2,6 and α has residues 3,5. The elements
  2−τα, (2−τ)+τα, 2+(−1+τ)α, and (1+τ)+(1−τ)α have absolute
  norm 7 and vanish at four distinct residue pairs. All four primes
  are principal.

This exhausts all primes that can divide representatives of norm ≤8,
proving h(A₃)=1. There is one GL lattice type for each trace.

## D19-L2: complete unit groups, with proved boxes

Write x=u+vr, u=a+bτ, v=c+dτ. Relative conjugation σ fixes K. A unit
has relative norm ±1 and integral inverse ±σx.

| Order | Expanding unit ε | Relative norm | |ε|² | Strict coefficient norm bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A₂ | (5+3τ)+(8−3τ)i | −1 | 170+39√19 | 171/2 |
| A₃ | 10+(−3−4τ)α | −1 | 151+20√57 | 304/3 |

Each is checked exactly. If E=|ε|>1, multiplication by an integral power
of ε reduces every unit to 1≤|x|<E. Since |σx|=|x|⁻¹, solving for u,v
bounds both squared moduli strictly by (E+E⁻¹)²/|r−σr|². These are the
bounds in the table. The norm formula forces |b|,|d|≤4 and |a|,|c|≤11
for A₂, ≤12 for A₃ (sqrt(171/2)+2<12 and sqrt(304/3)+2<13).
These are exhaustive boxes, not exploratory search cutoffs.

Use exact radical comparisons with

\[
 |u+vi|²=N(u)+N(v)+(bc-ad)\sqrt{19},
\]
\[
 |u+v\alpha|²=[2(N(u)+N(v))+2ac+ad+bc+10bd+(bc-ad)\sqrt{57}]/2.
\]

The strict coefficient filters give 12 norm-equation candidates for A₂ and
18 for A₃. The reduced units are exactly μ₄ and μ₆, respectively.
Therefore the entire unit groups are μ₄ ε₂^Z and μ₆ ε₃^Z. Both norm
images are {±1}. The replay checks all candidates, integral inverses, and
the reduced sets; it uses no assert that disappears under Python -O.

## D19-L3: element classes, translations and centralizers

A GL orbit splits into SL orbits indexed by O× modulo the determinant
image of its centralizer. Both images are {±1}, so each gives one SL
orbit. The involution yields one PSL class. For order 3, J₃=(1,1;0,−1)
conjugates α to α⁻¹ and has determinant −1. Multiplying J₃ by the
matrix of ε₃ gives an SL conjugator: the inverse elements merge. There
is exactly one order-3 element class, hence two elliptic classes in total.

In both orders the norm-one units have first expanding generator ε²;
the primitive translations are their multiplication matrices. Their norms
are (170+39√19)²=57799+13260√19 and
(151+20√57)²=45601+6040√57. Minimality follows from L2.

For the involution, J₂=diag(1,−1) times the matrix of ε₂ has determinant
one, squares to −I, and anticommutes with the representative. It reverses
the axis and centralizes the involution in PSL. Together with the rotation
it generates a Klein four group. The finite orientation-preserving kernel
has order two and the orientation quotient at most two, so this is the
full maximal finite centralizer, q=4. For trace one, anticommutation is
impossible; the finite centralizer is μ₆/{±1}, q=3. The replay checks
matrix determinants, commutation, finite orders and subgroup closure.

The cylinder orbital integral in D2-L7 gives log N(T₀)/(4q sin²(π/m)).
This proves the displayed coefficient. Its values are protected by this
proof and the record-bound arithmetic replay, not by B<1: dropping a
positive contribution makes B look better, and that numerical gate cannot
detect an undercount. Independent matrix-trace coefficient checks provide
a separate code path for the primitive lengths.

## D19-L4: no closed geodesic inside the support

[SYSTOLES.md](SYSTOLES.md) proves the global trace cutoff |tr|>3 and the
complete coordinate box [-6,6]² for remaining traces. For d=19 the witness
is τ, with length acosh((5+√77)/4). The finite exact trace invariants and
strict Arb comparisons prove every other loxodromic trace at least this
long. The witness matrix (0,−1;1,τ) lies in SL₂(O). Thus absence of closed
geodesics below this systole follows from a rigorous trace lower bound,
not just an empty enumerated length list. The certificate support is
rounded down and checked against the lower systole endpoint.

The shared analytic formulas and full-line tails are recorded in
[ANALYTIC_DERIVATIONS.md](ANALYTIC_DERIVATIONS.md). A spectral certificate
requires the full Arb bound after subtracting h(i) to have upper endpoint
below one. This proof alone is an inventory theorem, not an optimization
or a formal proof-assistant verification.
